Agent-Based Modeling of Heterogeneous Traders in Commodity Markets

Fundamentalists, Chartists, and Noise Traders in Soybean Futures

Author

PAIC Econometrics Research Team

Published

February 17, 2026

Executive Summary

This study develops an Agent-Based Model (ABM) of the Soybean futures market featuring three types of heterogeneous traders: fundamentalists, chartists, and noise traders. By calibrating the model’s fundamental value to historical moving averages, we simulate price dynamics that reproduce key empirical features including fat-tailed return distributions and volatility clustering. The comparison between simulated and real return distributions provides insights into the endogenous mechanisms that drive commodity market dynamics.

NoteResearch Context

Agent-based modeling provides a complementary perspective to classical econometrics by modeling markets from the bottom up. Rather than imposing statistical assumptions on aggregate time series, ABMs generate emergent macro-level patterns from the micro-level interactions of heterogeneous agents (Farmer and Foley 2009).

1 Introduction

Traditional financial models assume representative agents with rational expectations operating in efficient markets. However, decades of empirical evidence reveal systematic deviations from these assumptions: excess volatility, fat-tailed return distributions, volatility clustering, and persistent autocorrelation in absolute returns (Cont 2001).

Agent-Based Models (ABMs) provide an alternative framework that can endogenously generate these stylized facts through the interaction of heterogeneous traders with bounded rationality (LeBaron 2006). The foundational work of Brock and Hommes (1998) demonstrated how the interaction between fundamentalists and chartists can produce complex price dynamics, while Lux and Marchesi (1999) showed that a simple multi-agent model can generate the scaling properties observed in real financial data.

1.1 Literature Review

The heterogeneous agent literature has a rich history in computational finance. Hommes (2006) provided a comprehensive survey of models featuring agents with different expectations and trading strategies. Kirman (1993) drew parallels between ant colony behavior and market dynamics, emphasizing the role of social interaction in forming equilibria.

In the commodity context, Farmer and Foley (2009) argued that agent-based models are essential for understanding the economy’s complex adaptive behavior, particularly in markets subject to supply shocks and weather events. The Mesa framework (Kazil, Masad, and Crooks 2020) has democratized ABM development in Python, making it accessible to a broader research community.

1.2 Study Objectives

  1. Build a heterogeneous trader ABM with fundamentalists, chartists, and noise traders
  2. Calibrate fundamental value from Soybean price moving averages
  3. Simulate price dynamics and compare with real data
  4. Analyze return distributions: overlay simulated vs. real histograms
  5. Demonstrate emergent volatility clustering from agent interactions

2 Methodology

2.1 Agent Types

2.1.1 Fundamentalists

Fundamentalists believe that the market price will revert to an intrinsic fundamental value \(v_t\):

\[ D_t^F = \phi(v_t - p_t) + \epsilon_t^F \]

where \(D_t^F\) is the excess demand from fundamentalists, \(\phi > 0\) is the speed of mean reversion, \(p_t\) is the current price, and \(\epsilon_t^F\) is idiosyncratic noise.

2.1.2 Chartists (Trend Followers)

Chartists extrapolate recent price trends:

\[ D_t^C = \chi(p_t - p_{t-\tau}) + \epsilon_t^C \]

where \(\chi > 0\) represents trend-following intensity and \(\tau\) is the lookback period.

2.1.3 Noise Traders

Noise traders generate random demand:

\[ D_t^N \sim N(0, \sigma_N^2) \]

2.2 Price Formation

The market-clearing price adjusts according to aggregate excess demand:

\[ p_{t+1} = p_t + \lambda\left(n_F D_t^F + n_C D_t^C + n_N D_t^N\right) \]

where \(\lambda\) is the market impact parameter and \(n_F\), \(n_C\), \(n_N\) are the proportions of each agent type.

2.3 Fundamental Value Estimation

We calibrate the fundamental value using a long-run moving average of observed prices:

\[ v_t = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=0}^{W-1} p_{t-i} \]

where \(W\) is the window length (we use 200 trading days).

3.3 ABM Implementation

Code 
class CommodityABM:
    """
    Agent-Based Model of a commodity market with three trader types:
    fundamentalists, chartists, and noise traders.
    """
    
    def __init__(self, initial_price, fundamental_values, 
                 n_fundamentalists=100, n_chartists=60, n_noise=40,
                 phi=0.02, chi=0.015, sigma_noise=0.008,
                 market_impact=0.5, seed=42):
        
        np.random.seed(seed)
        
        self.fundamental_values = fundamental_values
        self.phi = phi            # fundamentalist speed
        self.chi = chi            # chartist trend intensity
        self.sigma_noise = sigma_noise  # noise trader volatility
        self.market_impact = market_impact
        
        self.n_fund = n_fundamentalists
        self.n_chart = n_chartists
        self.n_noise = n_noise
        self.n_total = n_fundamentalists + n_chartists + n_noise
        
        self.w_fund = n_fundamentalists / self.n_total
        self.w_chart = n_chartists / self.n_total
        self.w_noise = n_noise / self.n_total
        
        # Price history
        self.prices = [initial_price]
        self.returns = []
        
        # Agent-specific noise scale
        self.sigma_fund = 0.003
        self.sigma_chart = 0.005
        
        # Chartist lookback
        self.tau = 5
    
    def step(self, t):
        """Execute one time step of the simulation."""
        p_current = self.prices[-1]
        
        # Fundamental value at time t
        if t < len(self.fundamental_values):
            v_t = self.fundamental_values[t]
        else:
            v_t = self.fundamental_values[-1]
        
        # Fundamentalist demand: mean-revert to fundamental
        fund_demand = self.phi * (v_t - p_current) + np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_fund)
        
        # Chartist demand: follow trend
        if len(self.prices) > self.tau:
            trend = (p_current - self.prices[-self.tau]) / self.prices[-self.tau]
            chart_demand = self.chi * trend + np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_chart)
        else:
            chart_demand = np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_chart)
        
        # Noise trader demand
        noise_demand = np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_noise)
        
        # Aggregate excess demand
        excess_demand = (
            self.w_fund * fund_demand +
            self.w_chart * chart_demand +
            self.w_noise * noise_demand
        )
        
        # Price update
        p_new = p_current * (1 + self.market_impact * excess_demand)
        p_new = max(p_new, 1.0)  # price floor
        
        log_return = np.log(p_new / p_current)
        
        self.prices.append(p_new)
        self.returns.append(log_return)
    
    def run(self, n_steps):
        """Run the simulation for n_steps."""
        for t in range(n_steps):
            self.step(t)
        
        return np.array(self.prices), np.array(self.returns)

5 Discussion

5.1 Emergent Properties

The ABM successfully reproduces several key stylized facts of financial time series:

  1. Fat-tailed distributions: The interaction between heterogeneous agents generates return distributions with excess kurtosis, even though individual agent noise is normally distributed (Lux and Marchesi 1999).

  2. Volatility clustering: Periods of high volatility emerge endogenously from the positive feedback dynamics of chartist behavior, which amplifies price movements and creates self-reinforcing volatility regimes (Brock and Hommes 1998).

  3. Mean reversion: The fundamentalist agents provide a stabilizing force that prevents prices from diverging indefinitely from fundamental value, consistent with the error-correction mechanisms observed in commodity markets.

5.2 Role of Agent Heterogeneity

The balance between agent types critically determines market dynamics:

  • Fundamentalist dominance: Markets exhibit faster mean reversion and lower volatility, approaching efficient market behavior.
  • Chartist dominance: Markets show momentum effects, excess volatility, and potentially unstable dynamics.
  • Noise trader proportion: Higher noise makes the market less efficient but more realistic in its statistical properties (Hommes 2006).

5.3 Limitations

  • Simple agent rules: Real traders use far more sophisticated strategies; our agents follow stylized decision rules.
  • No adaptation: Agent proportions are fixed; in reality, traders switch strategies based on performance (Brock and Hommes 1998).
  • Single asset: The model considers only one commodity; cross-market interactions are ignored.
  • No institutional structure: Order book dynamics, margin requirements, and position limits are not modeled.

6 Conclusions

This study demonstrates that a relatively simple ABM with three heterogeneous trader types can reproduce key empirical features of Soybean futures returns. Our findings support the view that:

  1. Fat-tailed return distributions emerge naturally from the interaction of heterogeneous agents, without requiring fat-tailed innovation distributions.
  2. Volatility clustering is a consequence of the positive feedback created by chartist (trend-following) behavior.
  3. Fundamentalist trading provides an anchoring mechanism that prevents prices from diverging excessively from intrinsic value.
  4. The ABM framework offers a complementary perspective to time series econometrics, providing micro-level explanations for macro-level statistical regularities.

Future extensions should incorporate adaptive switching between strategies, multi-asset interactions, and calibration using evolutionary estimation methods.

## Methodology

### Agent Types

#### Fundamentalists
Fundamentalists believe that the market price will revert to an intrinsic fundamental value $v_t$:

$$
D_t^F = \phi(v_t - p_t) + \epsilon_t^F
$$

where $D_t^F$ is the excess demand from fundamentalists, $\phi > 0$ is the speed of mean reversion, $p_t$ is the current price, and $\epsilon_t^F$ is idiosyncratic noise.

#### Chartists (Trend Followers)
Chartists extrapolate recent price trends:

$$
D_t^C = \chi(p_t - p_{t-\tau}) + \epsilon_t^C
$$

where $\chi > 0$ represents trend-following intensity and $\tau$ is the lookback period.

#### Noise Traders
Noise traders generate random demand:

$$
D_t^N \sim N(0, \sigma_N^2)
$$

### Price Formation

The market-clearing price adjusts according to aggregate excess demand:

$$
p_{t+1} = p_t + \lambda\left(n_F D_t^F + n_C D_t^C + n_N D_t^N\right)
$$

where $\lambda$ is the market impact parameter and $n_F$, $n_C$, $n_N$ are the proportions of each agent type.

### Fundamental Value Estimation

We calibrate the fundamental value using a long-run moving average of observed prices:

$$
v_t = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=0}^{W-1} p_{t-i}
$$

where $W$ is the window length (we use 200 trading days).

### ABM Implementation

```{python}
#| label: abm-implementation
#| code-fold: show

class CommodityABM:
    """
    Agent-Based Model of a commodity market with three trader types:
    fundamentalists, chartists, and noise traders.
    """
    
    def __init__(self, initial_price, fundamental_values, 
                 n_fundamentalists=100, n_chartists=60, n_noise=40,
                 phi=0.02, chi=0.015, sigma_noise=0.008,
                 market_impact=0.5, seed=42):
        
        np.random.seed(seed)
        
        self.fundamental_values = fundamental_values
        self.phi = phi            # fundamentalist speed
        self.chi = chi            # chartist trend intensity
        self.sigma_noise = sigma_noise  # noise trader volatility
        self.market_impact = market_impact
        
        self.n_fund = n_fundamentalists
        self.n_chart = n_chartists
        self.n_noise = n_noise
        self.n_total = n_fundamentalists + n_chartists + n_noise
        
        self.w_fund = n_fundamentalists / self.n_total
        self.w_chart = n_chartists / self.n_total
        self.w_noise = n_noise / self.n_total
        
        # Price history
        self.prices = [initial_price]
        self.returns = []
        
        # Agent-specific noise scale
        self.sigma_fund = 0.003
        self.sigma_chart = 0.005
        
        # Chartist lookback
        self.tau = 5
    
    def step(self, t):
        """Execute one time step of the simulation."""
        p_current = self.prices[-1]
        
        # Fundamental value at time t
        if t < len(self.fundamental_values):
            v_t = self.fundamental_values[t]
        else:
            v_t = self.fundamental_values[-1]
        
        # Fundamentalist demand: mean-revert to fundamental
        fund_demand = self.phi * (v_t - p_current) + np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_fund)
        
        # Chartist demand: follow trend
        if len(self.prices) > self.tau:
            trend = (p_current - self.prices[-self.tau]) / self.prices[-self.tau]
            chart_demand = self.chi * trend + np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_chart)
        else:
            chart_demand = np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_chart)
        
        # Noise trader demand
        noise_demand = np.random.normal(0, self.sigma_noise)
        
        # Aggregate excess demand
        excess_demand = (
            self.w_fund * fund_demand +
            self.w_chart * chart_demand +
            self.w_noise * noise_demand
        )
        
        # Price update
        p_new = p_current * (1 + self.market_impact * excess_demand)
        p_new = max(p_new, 1.0)  # price floor
        
        log_return = np.log(p_new / p_current)
        
        self.prices.append(p_new)
        self.returns.append(log_return)
    
    def run(self, n_steps):
        """Run the simulation for n_steps."""
        for t in range(n_steps):
            self.step(t)
        
        return np.array(self.prices), np.array(self.returns)

print("ABM class defined successfully.")
print(f"Agent proportions: Fund={100/200*100:.0f}%, Chart={60/200*100:.0f}%, Noise={40/200*100:.0f}%")
```

## Discussion

### Emergent Properties

The ABM successfully reproduces several key stylized facts of financial time series:

1. **Fat-tailed distributions**: The interaction between heterogeneous agents generates return distributions with excess kurtosis, even though individual agent noise is normally distributed [@lux1999scaling].

2. **Volatility clustering**: Periods of high volatility emerge endogenously from the positive feedback dynamics of chartist behavior, which amplifies price movements and creates self-reinforcing volatility regimes [@brock1998heterogeneous].

3. **Mean reversion**: The fundamentalist agents provide a stabilizing force that prevents prices from diverging indefinitely from fundamental value, consistent with the error-correction mechanisms observed in commodity markets.

### Role of Agent Heterogeneity

The balance between agent types critically determines market dynamics:

- **Fundamentalist dominance**: Markets exhibit faster mean reversion and lower volatility, approaching efficient market behavior.
- **Chartist dominance**: Markets show momentum effects, excess volatility, and potentially unstable dynamics.
- **Noise trader proportion**: Higher noise makes the market less efficient but more realistic in its statistical properties [@hommes2006heterogeneous].

### Limitations

- **Simple agent rules**: Real traders use far more sophisticated strategies; our agents follow stylized decision rules.
- **No adaptation**: Agent proportions are fixed; in reality, traders switch strategies based on performance [@brock1998heterogeneous].
- **Single asset**: The model considers only one commodity; cross-market interactions are ignored.
- **No institutional structure**: Order book dynamics, margin requirements, and position limits are not modeled.

## Conclusions

This study demonstrates that a relatively simple ABM with three heterogeneous trader types can reproduce key empirical features of Soybean futures returns. Our findings support the view that:

1. Fat-tailed return distributions emerge naturally from the interaction of heterogeneous agents, without requiring fat-tailed innovation distributions.
2. Volatility clustering is a consequence of the positive feedback created by chartist (trend-following) behavior.
3. Fundamentalist trading provides an anchoring mechanism that prevents prices from diverging excessively from intrinsic value.
4. The ABM framework offers a complementary perspective to time series econometrics, providing micro-level explanations for macro-level statistical regularities.

Future extensions should incorporate adaptive switching between strategies, multi-asset interactions, and calibration using evolutionary estimation methods.
